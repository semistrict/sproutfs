# Four words — 2026-09-18

**Status: done**, the fifth word, *lineage*, included: added on 2026-09-19 and
gone from the code, the documents, the plans and the talk.

Reviewing the talk on 2026-09-18 found four places where the code and the
documents say something the reader has to decode. Each is a rename or a
rewording across the whole repository — code, comments, tests, documents and
plans — and then the talk follows.

## "frame" is "resident page"

The pager's physical 2 MiB of host memory is called a *frame*, a *resident
frame* or a *private frame*. It is a page — the same 2 MiB, the same unit — in
one of two states the reader already knows: **resident**, in host memory, and
possibly shared by every region whose page has that lineage identity; or
**private**, dirtied by one guest and owned by one region. So:

- *resident frame*, *frame* (of memory) → **resident page**;
- *private frame* → **private page**;
- *sealed frames* → **sealed pages**; *the frames it holds* → **the pages it
  holds**; *frame leases* → **resident page leases**, and so on through the
  identifiers: `frameLeases`, `heldFrames`, `needsPrivateFrame`,
  `droppedPageFrames`, `DroppedPageFrames`, `stallFrame`, the test names.
- The *arena* keeps its name: it is the memfd the resident pages live in, and
  its *slots* are the places a resident page can be.

The wire protocol's *frame* — `ReceivedFrame`, `ErrMalformedFrame`,
`encodeFrame`, `frameConn`, the proto file `frame.proto`, and the Rust
`Frame` of the control protocol — is a message frame, the ordinary word for
it, and it stays.

## "instant" is "pause", or "fork point"

The documents say *a checkpoint's instant*, *the fork instant*, *the instants
taken on a VM*. What is meant is either the **pause** — the stop, save, seal,
resume that both a checkpoint and a fork point begin with — or the **fork
point** itself, the pause a fork was taken at, which is already the noun the
code uses (`ForkPoint`). So *instant* goes: *a checkpoint's instant is
separable from its upload* → *a checkpoint is a pause and an upload, and only
the pause is on the latency path*; *the fork instant* → *the fork point*; *the
instants taken on it* → *the fork points taken on it*; test names likewise.
Plain-English uses that mean "moment" ("at the instant of the loss") may stay
where no better word reads well, but prefer *moment*.

## "two planes" is said plainly

*Two-plane store*, *the data plane*, *the metadata plane* describe a
checkpoint that is **its data — pages and the VMM state, in as many parts as
they fill — and one index object, written last**, whose create-if-absent PUT is
the commit. Say that. The words *part*, *index object* and *root* already carry
the structure; *plane* adds nothing and goes from `docs/`, `plans/` (the
histories may keep it where they quote the decision) and every comment.

## Decision 2 is stated, not contrasted

`docs/architecture.md`'s second decision says sharing is *through lineage,
without content hashes or byte-equality deduplication*. The contrast is history.
The decision is: **every page has one name — the checkpoint that published it
— and a fork inherits its parent's names; a page with a name is referenced,
never copied, in the store, in host memory and on the wire.** Reword it there,
in `docs/context.md`'s *Lineage identity*, and wherever a comment argues
against content addressing rather than stating the rule.

## How it is proved

Nothing changes behaviour: `just check` is green before and after, and no test
is added or removed — only renamed where its name carried a word. `rg -i frame`
over Go outside `vmmigrate`'s wire code and the Rust control protocol finds
nothing; `rg -i instant` finds only the plain-English uses the plan allows;
`rg -i plane` finds only histories under `plans/`.

## "lineage" goes (added 2026-09-19)

The word carries three meanings and adds nothing to any of them.

- **lineage identity** is a page's identity — `(checkpoint, volume, page)`, the
  name of the bytes a range reads. Say **page identity**; where the sentence is
  already about a page, **identity** alone.
- **a lineage** — *the pinned lineage*, *the lineage a deleted VM leaves behind*,
  *no lineage reads through it* — is the set of checkpoints a VM or a fork reads
  through. Say what is meant: **the checkpoints it inherits**, **the pinned
  checkpoints and everything their roots name**, **no descendant reads through
  it**. Do not coin a replacement noun.
- **sharing by lineage**, **share through lineage** → **sharing by identity**.

Identifiers follow: the `lineage` type and variables of that name become
`identity` (or `pageIdentity` where `identity` is taken), `populationLineage`,
`forkLineage`, `inheritedLineage`, `lineageOf` likewise, and the test names that
carry the word are renamed to say what they test. `VM`'s own "one lineage of
checkpoints" in `docs/context.md` becomes "one series of checkpoints".

Proved as the other four were: no behaviour changes, no test added or removed,
`just check` green, and `rg -i lineage` over the repository outside
`third_party/` and this plan finds nothing.
