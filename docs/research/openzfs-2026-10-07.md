# OpenZFS: what it does with a slow device — 2026-10-07

OpenZFS at fc15a2fbd (CDDL, OSI-approved), cloned at `~/src/openzfs`. Read for
ideas: sproutfs treats the object store the way a filesystem treats a spinning
disk. It is slow per operation, cheap per byte in bulk, and should be written
in large batches and read through caches. ZFS was built for that device. Paths
below are in the OpenZFS tree.

## Writes are batched into transaction groups

ZFS gathers every change into a transaction group and writes it out at once,
copy on write. A block is never overwritten in place: the new copy goes to new
space and the tree of block pointers above it is rewritten up to the root. A
sproutfs checkpoint is the same thing: dirty pages are published as new
objects, and the control record's selection is the new root.

## A block pointer carries its size, its checksum and its birth

`blkptr_t` (`include/sys/spa.h:389`) is 128 bytes: up to three addresses
(DVAs), logical and physical size (so compression is per block), a 256-bit
checksum, and the birth transaction group. The birth is what everything below
uses. A sproutfs page's identity, the checkpoint that published its bytes, is
the same field.

## A write smaller than a block rewrites the block

`dmu_write_impl` (`module/zfs/dmu.c:1451`) fills a block it covers whole, and
for a partial one calls `dmu_buf_will_dirty`, which reads the old block and
writes a whole new one. A 4 KiB write into a 128 KiB record writes 128 KiB.
This is the 2 MiB problem at a smaller size. ZFS does not solve it with
extents. It makes the block size a per-dataset property, `recordsize`, from
512 B to 16 MiB, and databases set it to their page size. Compression covers
part of the waste, as it does for sproutfs.

For sproutfs this suggests the cheap form of the 4 KiB question: a per-volume
storage page size, chosen per volume, with compression, before any extent
format.

## Snapshots free space by birth time, with no scan

`dsl_dataset_block_kill` (`module/zfs/dsl_dataset.c:258`, the rule at 303) runs
when a block is overwritten or deleted. If the block was born after the latest
snapshot, nothing else can reference it and it is freed at once. Otherwise it
goes on the dataset's deadlist, keyed by birth (`module/zfs/dsl_deadlist.c:454`).
Deleting a snapshot walks the deadlists of it and of the next snapshot and
frees the blocks born after the previous snapshot (`dsl_deadlist_move_bpobj`,
line 924). Space is never found by walking the tree.

sproutfs has no collector, and pins are permanent. Page identities already
record birth. A deadlist per checkpoint, written when a later checkpoint
replaces a page, would let a collector free a checkpoint's pages by reading
lists, not by walking every index. Forks are ZFS clones, whose own livelists
(`dsl_dataset.c:293`) track what they replaced since their origin.

## The intent log makes fsync fast without waiting for a group

The ZIL (`include/sys/zil.h:40`) is a chain of log blocks per dataset. An fsync
writes the records since the last group to it and waits only for that write.
The next group makes them redundant, and the log is trimmed. This is the
design of TASK-104.

How a write is logged (`zil_write_state`, `module/zfs/zil.c:2203`):

- With a separate log device (SLOG), the data is always copied into the log.
- Without one, a write of at least 32 KiB (`zfs_immediate_write_sz`) that
  covers at least half a block is written to its final place, and the log
  holds only its block pointer (`WR_INDIRECT`). Smaller writes are copied.
- Past 64 MiB in one commit (`zil_slog_bulk`, line 135), log writes to a SLOG
  drop to async priority so one large fsync does not starve the others.

After a crash, the log is claimed at import (`zh_claim_txg`, `zil.c:462`)
before it is replayed, so no block it names is reused. That is the fencing
step of the sproutfs plan, where a recovery reads only entries the control
record names.

## What applies

- **The journal (TASK-104)** matches the ZIL with a SLOG. One lesson to carry:
  a large flush might write its pages straight into a checkpoint part and log
  only where they are, as `WR_INDIRECT` does, instead of copying them twice.
- **The 2 MiB storage page** is ZFS's recordsize problem. ZFS's answer is a
  per-dataset size plus compression, not extents.
- **The missing collector** has a known design: birth times on every pointer
  and deadlists per snapshot.
- **Caching** maps to the ARC (memory) and L2ARC (a fast device), which are the
  page cache's memory and disk tiers.
